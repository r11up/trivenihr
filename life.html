Our people

A company is its people

Recruitment is demanding work. Files run late, embassies change requirements, and somebody is always waiting on an answer. The way we keep a team together through that is simple: look after them, mark the good days, and promote the people who do the work.

Annual Celebration

Once a year the whole company stops work, cuts a cake and marks another year together. It is the one day the entire team — head office and branch staff — is in the same room.

Employee Birthdays

Every birthday is celebrated at the office. Cake, the whole floor around one table, and nobody working through their own birthday unnoticed.

Award & Recognition Ceremony

We give awards to the people who earned them — best performer, longest service, the colleague everyone else relies on. Recognition is public, and it is specific.

Annual Company Tour

Every year the company travels together, and the itinerary usually includes an adventure programme that a good number of the team actually go through with.

Annual Picnic Programme

A company picnic every year, usually in the first quarter. Families welcome, food cooked on site, and a day away from the paperwork.

Promotions from Within

We promote our own. Staff who grow into more responsibility get the title and the pay that go with it — several of our senior people started in operations roles.

The office

Where wework

  • A core team of more than 50 people working from our own four-floor building in Samakhushi — three floors of offices and three flats.
  • Our own kitchen. Breakfast and lunch are cooked in-house for staff every working day.
  • In the heart of the capital. Next to the Nepal Police Cyber Bureau, the Nepal Police Dog Training centre and the Nepal Police Music Association, close to the Nepal Police armed facility.
  • Everything within reach. Ten minutes from the city centre, with the Department of Passports and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs a short ride away — which is exactly why paperwork moves quickly here.

It is an ordinary thing to mention a kitchen on a company website. We mention it because a hot lunch, cooked in the building, is the sort of detail that tells you how a company treats the people who work there.

Growing here

Promotion,not just a job

We would rather promote someone who already knows how our files work than hire a stranger into a senior seat. Staff who take on more responsibility get the title and the pay that go with it, and we invest in training so that they can.

  • Internal promotion to supervisory and director-level roles
  • Personality development and skills training for our own staff
  • Awards and public recognition for standout performance
  • Breakfast and lunch cooked in-house, every working day

The staff at Triveni HR are highly qualified with wide experience in projects and human resources. A professional, dedicated team is the main strength of this organisation, and we invest time, finance and ideas in the people who work here.

Corporate social responsibility

Recruitment takes from a community

Every worker we place abroad leaves a household and a village behind. A company that makes its living from that movement of people owes something back to the places those people come from. We give it with our own money and our own time — and we work with partners who know the ground better than we do.

Partnerships

Working with Rotaryand non-profits

We partner with the Rotary Club and non-profit institutions such as Fulcrum on programmes designed to raise living standards in Nepalese communities — health, maternal care, education and welfare.

One recent example is the Maternal Kits Distribution Program for New Born Babies and Mothers, organised jointly by the Rotary Club of Tinkune Kathmandu and the Children Care Club, which our founder took part in directly.

  • Maternal and newborn care kits for mothers in need
  • Donations to community health and welfare causes
  • Ongoing partnerships with Rotary clubs and non-profit institutions across Nepal
Partner with us
Education

Supporting schoolsin rural Nepal

We run education and career-awareness programmes in government secondary schools outside the capital — the districts most of our candidates come from. Students hear honest information about skills, trades and what working abroad actually involves, from people who do it for a living.

  • Career and skills awareness sessions in government schools
  • Support for rural school programmes and learning materials
  • Honest guidance on foreign employment — including its risks

Telling a sixteen-year-old the truth about overseas work is not a marketing exercise. It is how you stop them being exploited by someone less careful.
