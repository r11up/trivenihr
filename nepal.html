About Nepal

The country

Nepal ata glance

Nepal is a landlocked country in South Asia, lying between India and China along the southern slope of the Himalaya. Kathmandu is the capital and the centre of government, commerce and international travel. The country is divided into seven provinces and 77 districts.

Nepali is the official language, and English is widely used in education, business and government. A large share of the workforce has at least basic English, and many workers returning from the Gulf also have working Arabic.

Foreign employment is a central part of Nepal’s economy. Remittances sent home by Nepalese working abroad account for a substantial share of national income, and the government actively regulates the sector to protect workers.

The workforce

Why employers hire Nepalese workers

A young labour force

Nepal has a large working-age population and a steady flow of young people entering the labour market each year, which makes bulk recruitment realistic at short notice.

A reputation for reliability

Nepalese workers have a long-standing reputation for discipline, endurance and loyalty — a reputation built over generations, including in international military service.

Experience in the Gulf

Hundreds of thousands of Nepalese already work across the GCC and Malaysia. Many candidates are returnees who know the climate, the sites and the working culture.

Trained and tested

Government-accredited training centres run skills and pre-departure programmes. Trade tests can be arranged in Kathmandu before selection is confirmed.

Language

English is taught throughout schooling and is widely understood. Hindi is broadly intelligible, which helps on mixed South Asian sites.

A regulated process

Recruitment is governed by the Foreign Employment Act, 2064 (2007). Working with a licensed agency protects both the employer and the worker.

Regulation

How foreign employment is governed

Who regulates recruitment from Nepal?

The Department of Foreign Employment, under the Ministry of Labour, Employment and Social Security, licenses recruitment agencies, approves demand letters and issues the labour permits that every Nepalese worker needs before departing for work abroad.

What law applies?

The Foreign Employment Act, 2064 (2007) and the Foreign Employment Regulation set out how recruitment must be conducted, what may be charged to workers, and the obligations of licensed agencies. Directives issued by the Government of Nepal from time to time also apply.

Why does the demand letter need embassy attestation?

Attestation by the Nepalese diplomatic mission accredited to your country confirms that your company exists, that the job offer is genuine and that the stated terms are real. The Department of Foreign Employment will not approve recruitment without it.

What is a labour permit?

It is the approval each worker must hold before leaving Nepal for employment abroad. It links the worker to an approved employer and an approved contract, and it is what makes the placement lawful on the Nepalese side.

How do I verify that an agency is licensed?

Ask for the licence number and check it against the Department of Foreign Employment’s records. Triveni HR’s licence number is 1174/073/074 and our certificates are published on our licence page.
